We drove up on Friday evening and reached the cabin just as the last light left the water. The lake was perfectly still, and the far ridges went from blue to grey to nothing while we unloaded the car.


![[mountain-lake.png|wrap-left|330]]
Saturday started early. The mist sat low over the water until the sun cleared the eastern ridge, and then it lifted all at once, as if someone had pulled a sheet off the lake. We took the canoe out along the north shore, past the old boathouse and the line of pines that leans out over the water. Nobody else was out yet, and the only sounds were the paddles and a pair of loons somewhere behind us.

By noon the wind had picked up enough to make the crossing interesting, so we pulled in at the little beach below the ranger station and ate lunch on the rocks. The water was colder than it looked.

## The old lighthouse

![[lighthouse.png|wrap-right|180]]
On Sunday we followed the coast road to the lighthouse at the point. It was built in 1871 and still runs every night, although the keeper's cottage is now a small museum with nets, floats and a logbook that someone kept for forty years in the same neat handwriting.

The path to the top of the cliff is steep but short. From there you can see the whole bay, the ferry crossing to the island, and on a clear day the mountains we had paddled under the day before.
